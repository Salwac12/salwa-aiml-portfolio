Research note 01 · AI + Cybersecurity

Why email encryption needs more than a lock icon

When we think about secure email, it is easy to focus on whether a connection uses encryption. But the strength of that protection depends on much more than a simple on/off signal. Servers negotiate versions of TLS, choose cipher suites and present certificates. Even an available STARTTLS upgrade is not useful if a session continues in an unsafe state.

The MailShield AI concept begins with captured network traffic. Rather than asking a person to inspect every packet, it proposes reconstructing the relevant email sessions and highlighting the cryptographic details that matter. From there, deterministic checks can identify known weaknesses, while anomaly detection can help surface patterns worth investigating.

An important lesson in this approach is that automation should make evidence easier to understand, not replace judgment. A good security report should explain what was observed, which records support the finding and why a configuration may be risky. The proposed system also emphasizes analyzing handshake metadata without needing to read private message contents.

That combination of privacy, explainability and practical next steps is what makes this kind of AI-assisted security analysis interesting to study.

Research note 02 · AI + Sustainability

Can artificial intelligence help us spot greenwashing?

Terms like “eco-friendly” and “sustainable” appear everywhere, but what information would actually support those claims? A label by itself cannot explain the source of raw materials, the environmental impact of manufacturing or whether a certification is valid. That is the problem explored by the EcoTrust AI concept.

The proposed system brings together different kinds of evidence: product details, supplier documents, certifications and production data. Optical character recognition and natural-language processing could help extract information from documents, while comparison rules and machine learning could flag missing data or contradictions. Human verification remains a key step, especially when the claims affect people's purchasing decisions.

A useful result would not simply be a mysterious score. It would show which claims have supporting evidence, which need more review and how the final rating was reached. The concept also includes a QR-accessible sustainability profile to make findings easier for consumers to understand.

For me, the broader question raised by this research is how technology can increase trust without making exaggerated promises of certainty. Reliable AI needs good data, clear explanations and accountability.
